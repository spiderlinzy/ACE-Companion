ACE Case File

An end-to-end case study of a compliant enterprise RAG knowledge assistant — how regulation becomes objectives, controls, and auditable evidence.

01

The System at a Glance

A mid-sized financial-technology firm runs an internal RAG knowledge assistant for its workforce. This page follows that single system end to end.

Illustrative case study. All organization names, data, test results and metrics are synthetic and simulated to demonstrate the method; no real organization or experimental finding is represented.

System profile

FinTechCo, a mid-sized financial-technology company, deploys an internal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) knowledge assistant for 2,000 employees through its enterprise chat platform.

Employees ask natural-language questions about internal compliance policies, product documentation and customer-service knowledge. The assistant retrieves relevant document chunks, produces a grounded answer and cites its sources.

02

Architecture and Trust Boundaries

Two trust models run through the design: PTB separates the prompt layers, and FTH grades the confidence of each document source.

End-to-end architecture: input detection, RAG retrieval, LLM inference, output validation and human review, under continuous monitoring.

Seven layers, from the user and application layers through orchestration, RAG, model, output and monitoring.

Prompt isolation

The system prompt is physically separated from user context. Input and retrieved documents sit in a marked “untrusted content” block whose instructions are treated as data, not commands.

No retrieval, no answer

For compliance questions, if confidence is below threshold the assistant declines and points to the compliance team, rather than filling gaps with parametric knowledge.

03

Risk Register and Grading

Ten risks are identified across adversarial, implementation, operational and governance categories, then graded by likelihood and impact.

Risk acceptance criteria

A High risk needs at least two independent control layers before approval; Medium-High needs one fully implemented control with effectiveness evidence; Medium needs a documented mitigation plan; Low is accepted with monitoring.

07

Evidence Package and Acceptance Decision

The CISO and Head of Compliance review a ten-item evidence package before production approval.

Evidence package for production approval

    Decision: Conditional approval for production deployment

    All High risks carry two independent control layers with passing evidence, thresholds are met in the simulated results, and residual risks are formally accepted.

    • Monthly rerun of access-control and PII test suites
    • Quarterly red-team testing reviewed by the security committee
    • Immediate rollback if injection ASR exceeds 10% or PII leakage exceeds 0%
    • Model updates require a full regression test before deployment
    • A 90-day post-deployment review validates production against test results
    Mapping to the EU AI Act: The same evidence chain maps onto Act obligations including Art. 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 43, 50, 72 and 73. Engineering relevance and legal applicability are kept separate: this internal assistant is not assumed to be high-risk, so not every mapped obligation legally applies.
