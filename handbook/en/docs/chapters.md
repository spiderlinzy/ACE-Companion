# Chapter Guide

The book *AI Compliance Engineering: From Regulation to Evidence — A Methodology for Trustworthy and Ethical AI Systems* is organized into an introduction, four parts totaling 16 chapters, an epilogue and Appendices A–D. This page is a structural map; **the full book text is not published on this site** — complete argumentation, figures and the end-to-end case are in the formal publication.

## Introduction

- **Introduction**: why compliance must move from “post-hoc audit” to “built-in engineering,” and the book’s methodological backbone.

## Part I · Theoretical Frameworks

- **Chapter 1 · AI Compliance Engineering**: disciplinary positioning, four-tier risk grading and an engineering view of compliance.
- **Chapter 2 · CTF and RCE Frameworks**: structure and functions of the Compliance Translation Framework (CTF) and the Risk–Control–Evidence loop (RCE).
- **Chapter 3 · PTB and FTH Trust-Boundary Models**: the five-level models and violation rules of the Prompt Trust Boundary and the Fact Trust Hierarchy.

## Part II · Core Security Controls

- **Chapter 4 · Data Compliance Foundation**: governance, privacy and hygiene of training and retrieval data.
- **Chapter 5 · Prompt-Injection Defense**: layered defense and the “validate output first” design.
- **Chapter 6 · RAG Architecture Security**: data hygiene, permissions and grounding reliability in retrieval-augmented generation.
- **Chapter 7 · Hallucination Mitigation**: source constraints, fact-checking and output-confidence management.
- **Chapter 8 · Agent Permission Control**: tool-call allowlists, least privilege and mandatory human confirmation.

## Part III · Verification and Operational Assurance

- **Chapter 9 · Benchmarking**: metric systems, test-set design and capacity-performance evaluation.
- **Chapter 10 · Red Teaming**: attacker-perspective case design, rating and the regression closed loop.
- **Chapter 11 · Explainability and Fairness**: decision-explanation mechanisms and algorithmic-bias assessment.
- **Chapter 12 · Continuous Monitoring and Incident Response**: drift detection, alerting, incident grading and post-mortems.

## Part IV · Organizational Implementation

- **Chapter 13 · Supply-Chain Security**: model and component provenance, provider-status triggers and downstream information handoff.
- **Chapter 14 · Compliance Governance Platform**: governance-platform capabilities and the organizational maturity model.
- **Chapter 15 · Engineering Implementation Checklist and Roadmap**: phased sequencing, budget and alignment with the regulatory timeline.
- **Chapter 16 · End-to-End Case Study**: the framework and controls fully connected in financial risk-control / due-diligence scenarios.

## Epilogue and Appendices

- **Epilogue**: transferability of the methodology and directions for future revision.
- **Appendix A · Verification Methodology**: operational details of benchmarking, red teaming and statistical rules.
- **Appendix B · Ethical Boundary Cases**: case discussions of tensions such as efficiency vs. fairness and autonomy vs. automation.
- **Appendix C · Methodology Limitations**: full statement of validation status, metric calibration, regulatory scope and organizational applicability.
- **Appendix D · Regulatory Evidence Index**: the complete Obligation → Evidence Artifact → Method mapping, OWASP crosswalk and evidence templates (the public selected edition on this site: [Regulation–Evidence Map](regulation-map.md) and [Evidence Artifact Templates](evidence-templates.md)).
- **Glossary / References**: term definitions and cited sources.
